# Mathematical Pipeline Notebook

Date: 4 November 2026. Scope: work implemented so far.

## 1. The research question

Can a partial gluing history be replaced by a smaller marked state without losing the information needed to determine a specified outcome after future gluings?

Write h for a history, S(h) for its state, and f for a compatible continuation. The desired property is

$$S(h)=S(h') \Longrightarrow I(h\cdot f)=I(h'\cdot f),$$

whenever both completed objects belong to the target class. Here I is a chosen invariant, currently integral homology. This implication is a research specification. Our finite experiments test instances of it; they do not prove it for arbitrary histories.

Different goals require different states. Deciding manifold legality, computing homology, and deciding homeomorphism are separate tasks.

## 2. Global roadmap

Implemented experimental route:

1. Fix a finite combinatorial skeleton and the labeled face pairs.
2. Enumerate affine vertex bijections on the paired triangular faces.
3. Reconstruct the quotient and its local links from the complete history.
4. Reject objects failing the target local topology or orientability conditions.
5. Construct integer chain complexes and compute certified homology.
6. Extract marked boundary data Ce and Cv.
7. Exhibit histories with equal Ce/Cv but different completed homology.
8. Add the boundary-to-interior H1 inclusion map to the state.
9. Compare genuinely distinct histories sharing the enhanced state.
10. Apply identical continuations and compare completed homology.
11. Save inputs, decisions, certificates, comparisons and counterexamples.

Additional implemented check: for the one-tetrahedron family, predict H1 using boundary data and the inclusion map, without reading the completed history.

Future route, not completed: general state-only updates; canonical map equivalence for arbitrary finitely generated abelian groups; symmetry-aware history deduplication; larger families; tree-decomposition dynamic programming; complexity and resource analysis; selected formal verification.

## 3. Objects and notation

Let T be a disjoint union of n labeled tetrahedra. Each tetrahedron has vertices 0,1,2,3. A face map

$$t:abc\longrightarrow s:p(a)p(b)p(c)$$

is the affine map determined by the three displayed vertex images. Extend p to a permutation of all four labels by mapping the omitted vertex to the omitted vertex. The quotient is X=T/~, where ~ is generated by all specified face maps.

These are generalized triangulations: distinct faces of the same tetrahedron may be paired, and quotient vertices and edges may coincide. They need not be strict simplicial complexes.

A face-pairing graph has one node per tetrahedron and one edge per paired face pair. A self-pair contributes a loop. A closed pairing gives a 4-regular multigraph, counting a loop twice. This graph is distinct from the quotient's 1-skeleton and from a Cayley graph.

## 5. Equivalence closure and signed identifications

Vertex identifications generate an equivalence relation on local vertices (t,v). Its equivalence classes are the quotient vertices.

Orient a local edge by increasing labels a<b. If p maps it in increasing order, impose e=+e'; otherwise impose e=-e'. Signed closure detects a contradiction e=-e, meaning an edge has been identified with its reverse. Such a contradiction is a rejection witness.

An implementation may use graph connected components or signed union-find. These are ways to compute the same finite equivalence constraints; neither is a topological theorem by itself.

## 6. Vertex links and manifold legality

Cut off a small triangle at each tetrahedron corner. Face gluings induce edge gluings among these triangles. The resulting component at a quotient vertex is its link Lk(v).

The checker tests triangle connectivity, side incidence, every link vertex's local interval/circle neighborhood, orientability, boundary components and Euler characteristic.

Surface classification theorem: a connected closed orientable surface has chi=2-2g. Therefore a verified connected closed orientable surface with chi=2 is S2. Chi=2 alone is not our complete test.

For a genuine compact triangulated 3-manifold, interior vertex links are S2 and boundary vertex links are D2. The generalized-triangulation criterion also excludes reversed self-edges. See Burton and Pettersson, Definition 4. The paper's partial-3-manifold condition is more permissive than requiring every current link to be a disk or sphere: punctured spheres may occur. Our enhanced-state experiments use the stricter manifold-with-boundary prefix filter. The 198 excluded two-tetrahedron prefixes must be interpreted relative to that filter.

Example: 103-132 has spherical vertex links but reversed self-edges. It is rejected. Example: 031-213 has a nonorientable closed chi=0 link, a Klein bottle, and is rejected.

## 7. Global orientability

Choose a tetrahedron orientation sign epsilon_t in {+1,-1}. A full vertex permutation p on a gluing t->s imposes

$$\epsilon_s=-\operatorname{sgn}(p)\epsilon_t.$$

Solve these sign constraints on every component. A contradictory cycle means the gluing is not globally orientable. Link orientability and global orientability are separate checks.

The target completed objects in these experiments are closed, connected, orientable 3-manifolds. No unpaired faces is checked separately from manifold legality.

## 8. Integer chain complexes

Choose oriented quotient-cell representatives. Let Ck=Z^{c_k} and Bk represent the cellular boundary operator:

$$0\to C_3\xrightarrow{B_3}C_2\xrightarrow{B_2}C_1\xrightarrow{B_1}C_0\to0.$$

Use the alternating boundary formula

$$\partial[v_0,\ldots,v_k]=\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^j[v_0,\ldots,\widehat{v_j},\ldots,v_k].$$

Transport each term into its oriented quotient basis. Check B1 B2=0 and B2 B3=0 exactly.

Homology is

$$H_k(X;\mathbb Z)=\ker B_k/\operatorname{im}B_{k+1}.$$

Smith normal form theorem: integer unimodular U,V give UAV=D with positive nonzero diagonal entries d1|d2|...|dr. A unimodular matrix has determinant +/-1 and preserves the integer lattice.

Our calculation first finds an integral kernel basis, expresses the incoming boundary in that basis, then takes its Smith quotient. This matters: coker B2 is generally not H1 when B1 is nonzero.

Certificates record transformation identities, unimodularity, kernel coordinates and group invariant factors. Floating-point rank is not used to infer torsion.

## 9. Worked chain calculation: 013-123

First 012->013 gives {0},{1},{2,3}. Then 023->123 gives A={0,1}, B={2,3}. Both vertex links have (V,E,F)=(3,3,2), with all required surface checks satisfied.

With edge basis a=[01], b=[02], c=[23], the saved matrices are

$$B_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad B_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad B_3=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\end{pmatrix}.$$

Here b connects different vertices and is not a cycle. Ker B1 is generated by a,c, and im B2 is generated by c,a. Hence H1=0. All homology groups are (Z,0,0,Z). This homology calculation alone is not used to name the manifold.

## 10. Boundary state Ce and Cv

Cv partitions local vertices appearing on currently unpaired faces. Ce pairs boundary face-edge occurrences, recording whether their increasing-label orientations agree. Include the marked boundary face labels.

Ce/Cv describe the current interface, not every interior identification. Once all faces are paired, both can be empty although the quotient still has vertices, edges and nontrivial topology.

For 013-123 after the first gluing:

- Cv={ {0},{1},{2,3} }.
- 023/02 ~ 023/03 with equal direction.
- 123/12 ~ 123/13 with equal direction.
- 023/23 ~ 123/23 with equal direction.

Boundary configurations in the cited FPT work serve future legality decisions. Preserving that purpose does not automatically preserve a global invariant such as integral homology.

## 11. The information-loss witness

The first gluings 012->130 and 012->301 have identical marked Ce/Cv. Both partial objects have H1=Z. On their common boundary let x=[03], y=[13], z=[23]=x+y.

Their inclusion maps differ:

$$i_{130}(x)=2u,\ i_{130}(y)=u;\qquad i_{301}(x)=u,\ i_{301}(y)=2u.$$

The same completion 023->231 requires [02]=[23]. Since Ce gives [02]=-y, this imposes x+2y=0. The two histories therefore give 4u=0 and 5u=0, respectively. Completed H1 is Z/4 versus Z/5.

Conclusion: Ce/Cv alone do not determine completed homology in this family. This is a counterexample to that proposed information-preservation property, not to the original legality algorithm.

## 12. The enhanced state and its equality rule

$$S^+(M)=(C_e,C_v,H_1(M;\mathbb Z),i_*:H_1(\partial M;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(M;\mathbb Z)).$$

Build the boundary chain complex from Ce/Cv and boundary labels alone. Construct inclusion chain matrices Jk and verify

$$B_k^M J_k=J_{k-1}B_k^{\partial M}.$$

A chain map sends cycles to cycles and boundaries to boundaries, and therefore induces a well-defined homology homomorphism. Compute its matrix using certified integral homology coordinates.

Boundary markings remain fixed. For interior H1=Z the only automorphisms are multiplication by +/-1, so normalize the first nonzero map entry to be positive. Interior H1=0 has the unique map to the zero group. Arbitrary higher-rank or torsion-target map equivalence is not implemented.

The actual deterministic boundary basis for 130/301 is (x,z), not (x,y). Their normalized map rows are [2,3] and [1,3]. These encode the same distinction as [2,1] and [1,2] in the earlier basis.

Equality currently means equality in this fixed marked representation. It is not unmarked homeomorphism equivalence.

## 13. State-only H1 prediction in the one-tetrahedron family

Let K be the remaining boundary, q:K->Q its quotient under the last face map, and F=M union_K Q the completed quotient. K is a CW subcomplex of M. Using the corresponding homotopy-pushout/double-mapping-cylinder model, apply Mayer-Vietoris:

$$H_1(K)\xrightarrow{(i_*,-q_*)}H_1(M)\oplus H_1(Q)\to H_1(F)\to H_0(K)\to H_0(M)\oplus H_0(Q).$$

In our connected M,K,Q situation the last map sends 1 to (1,-1), hence is injective. Exactness yields

$$H_1(F)\cong\operatorname{coker}(i_*,-q_*).$$

This is an application of a standard theorem, not a newly discovered theorem. Disconnected interfaces require the H0 part to be retained and treated.

The predictor builds Q and q from the boundary state plus the continuation. It receives no original first gluing, interior chain matrices, or final homology truth.

For continuation 231 in basis (x,z), q*=[2,1]. The presentation matrices are

$$R_{130}=\begin{pmatrix}2&3\\-2&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad R_{301}=\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\-2&-1\end{pmatrix}.$$

Their determinants are 4 and 5, with entry gcd one, giving Smith factors (1,4) and (1,5).

Results: 9 predicted legal completions agree with full-history H1; 9 other eligible-prefix attempts are rejected for boundary-edge reversal. This implemented predictor is currently a one-tetrahedron component.

## 14. What the finite experiments establish

One tetrahedron: 36 assignments, 9 accepted. H1 distribution: zero in five labeled cases, Z/4 in two, Z/5 in two. Three eligible first histories form two basic-state groups but three enhanced-state groups. The original two homology counterexamples are separated. No distinct-history enhanced-state collision remains, so absence of further counterexamples there is vacuous as a compression test.

Two tetrahedra: 1296 assignments, 66 accepted; 216 three-gluing histories; 18 pass the selected strict prefix filter. Their enhanced states form nine groups, each shared by distinct labeled histories. Applying the six common continuations gives 54 pairwise comparisons; 27 have two accepted completions, all with equal H0 through H3. No homology counterexample was observed in those 27.

Do not count the other 27 comparisons as successful homology matches. Do not assume the paired histories remain distinct after relabeling. This experiment does not prove a general state-sufficiency theorem.

## 15. Verification levels and implementation limitations

The one-tetrahedron and enhanced-state components have dedicated independent checkers and exact certificate tests. At completion of that stage, the experiment suite had 82 passing tests.

The later two-tetrahedron extension is a prototype. It constructs its full histories and homology certificates, and cross-checks accepted objects with an existing signed-quotient implementation, including B2 Smith factors. It does not yet have an equally comprehensive standalone independent checker for all rejected cases, all inclusion maps and the entire comparison table. The previously reported 82 tests must not be described as full verification of this later prototype.

The two-tetrahedron inclusion adapter currently specializes the earlier routine by replacing its single-tetrahedron face enumeration in an isolated function namespace. This is a temporary implementation mechanism, not a mathematical construction. A clean parameterized implementation remains future engineering work.

Current accepted-case cross-checks are evidence, not a Lean proof. The full-history reconstruction remains the oracle for the two-tetrahedron collision experiment; no general state-only update pipeline has been completed.

## 16. Earlier Cayley-graph branch

This is a separate methodological baseline. For small groups, BFS provides exact word distances. Sound shortlex-decreasing rewrite rules prune words while retaining at least one shortest representative for each element: a shortlex-minimal shortest word cannot contain a reducible left-hand side.

Forbidden-pattern automata encode surviving words. A reachable live directed cycle certifies infinitely many accepted words. A finite live DAG permits longest-path computation. When sound reductions retain representatives and the surviving language has maximum word length U, every group element has a representative of length at most U, so diameter <= U.

D8 and D16 experiments attained U equal to BFS diameters 5 and 9 with the selected two relations. Ordinary relation pools also contain successful two-relation choices. This does not establish an intrinsic topological speedup. The Cayley graph and face-pairing graph are different objects, and no cube-diameter algorithm follows from the tetrahedral experiments.

## 17. What is not yet claimed

No new homeomorphism-classification theorem; no general homology-aware FPT algorithm; no proven asymptotic speedup; no four-by-four cube upper bound; no Lean formalization. Equal homology is not a homeomorphism certificate. Different labeled histories are not automatically different spaces.

The implemented contribution is a reproducible finite workflow: construct, validate, extract a state, find information-loss witnesses, enrich the state, and test common continuations against full-history truth.

## 18. Notebook vocabulary

History: ordered face maps already applied.
State: selected information retained from a history.
Continuation: face maps applied afterward.
Invariant: a mathematical quantity unchanged under an explicitly specified equivalence.
Certificate: finite data allowing a claim to be checked.
Counterexample: two histories satisfying the comparison premise but producing different target invariants.
Canonicalization: normalization of representation under a stated set of allowed basis changes; not a synonym for topological classification.
