Midterm Research Report
Fixed Graphs, Gluing Structures, and Topological Information
October 30, 2026
Abstract
This report summarizes my study of Cayley graph spectra, surface embeddings, and com-
binatorial constructions of topological spaces. Three main references provide complementary
tools: representation theory explains spectral information, rotation systems describe surface
embeddings, and spatial graph constructions address geometric realizability. More recent read-
ing motivates local link validation and boundary-state representations for tetrahedral gluings.
The current research direction is to determine what topological information can vary when the
underlying face-pairing graph remains ﬁxed.
1 Research Development
The project began with ﬁnite groups and Cayley graphs, then developed toward face systems,
surface topology, and diﬀerent notions of equivalence.
The main conceptual progression is
ﬁnite groups − → Cayley graphs − → face systems − → topological spaces .
The central observation is that these levels contain diﬀerent information. A group and generating
set determine a graph, but the graph does not determine which cycles are ﬁlled as faces. Likewise,
an algebraic invariant need not determine the entire space.
My current question is therefore:
When a combinatorial skeleton is ﬁxed, how much topological variation can diﬀerent
gluing structures produce, and which invariants detect that variation?

• Replace visually selected cycles with explicit rotation data and reproducible face tracing.

2.3 Barthel–Buccoliero: Geometric Realization
Reference. Barthel and Buccoliero, Constructing embedded surfaces for cellular embeddings of
leveled spatial graphs [3].
Main idea
This paper begins with a graph already embedded in three-dimensional space. It asks whether the
graph can lie on an embedded closed orientable surface whose complementary regions are open
disks.
Theorem 2.5 (Four-level existence result) . If a spatial graph admits a leveled embedding with at
most four levels, then it admits a cellular embedding in a closed orientable surface embedded in R3.
This restates Proposition 1 of the paper.
Application to my project
• Distinguish an abstract surface construction from its realization in three-dimensional space.
• Investigate whether a selected spatial realization of a Cayley graph admits a compatible cellular
surface.
• Use cylinder and punctured-surface attachments as geometric models for changing the surround-
ing surface.
• Check cellularity using the current face boundaries, rather than relying only on the original
drawing or spine.
Limitation. The construction concerns a speciﬁed spatial graph. It does not enumerate all
abstract face systems or automatically produce a minimum-genus surface. Geometric realization
remains an optional extension of the current ﬁnite gluing study.

• Record local identiﬁcations explicitly and reject constructions with invalid links.

Theorem 4.1 (Admissibility and treewidth) . For a connected 4-regular multigraph with n nodes
and treewidth at most k, the existence of a closed 3-manifold triangulation with that face-pairing
graph can be decided in O(nf (k)) time, given a suitable tree decomposition with O(n) bags.
This is an existence result from [ 4]. It does not directly provide an equally eﬃcient enumeration
or homeomorphism-classiﬁcation algorithm.
5 Current Research Question
For a ﬁxed face-pairing graph Γ, let MΓ,φdenote the space determined by gluing maps φ.
The main question is whether two gluing assignments can produce closed, connected, orientable
3-manifolds satisfying
Hj(MΓ,φ; Z) ∼= Hj(MΓ,ψ; Z) for every j,
while
MΓ,φ̸∼=homeo MΓ,ψ.
Related questions are:
• Can such a diﬀerence arise by changing only one face-gluing map?
• Can equal intermediate boundary states lead to diﬀerent ﬁnal homology?
• What additional information would a state need to retain a chosen topological invariant?
These are working research questions. Their novelty and answers have not been established.

9 Expected Outcome
The expected outcome is a small, reproducible study showing how ﬁxed combinatorial data can
support diﬀerent constructions and how selected invariants distinguish them.
The report will clearly separate known theorems, reconstructed arguments, ﬁnite computations,
numerical observations, and working hypotheses. A new general classiﬁcation theorem is not
required for completion.
